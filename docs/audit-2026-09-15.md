## A. Security

### A1 · `activity_series` could be written directly — **closed**

`0008` revoked the direct `UPDATE` on `activities` because it let an organizer write
`joined_count`, `status` and `waitlist_count` through PostgREST. `activity_series` kept a
`FOR ALL` policy over a full write grant, so everything `0008` forbids on a session was
available on the template that generates them: a post-hoc price change, and moving the time,
weekday and venue with people already on upcoming occurrences.

`0013` revokes `UPDATE` and routes changes through `update_series()` and `cancel_series()`.

**Verified after the fix:**

```
activities      | authenticated | DELETE,INSERT,SELECT
activity_series | authenticated | DELETE,INSERT,SELECT
```

The two now match. `INSERT` deliberately stays — creating is not the dangerous verb, and
`createSeries()` uses it; `12_series_mutations_test.sql` asserts creation still works, so a
migration that locked the door on the way in would fail rather than ship.

`update_series()` also propagates to future occurrences nobody has joined, which closes the
silent-divergence half: editing a series never touched the sessions generated from it and
nothing reconciled them.

### A1b · `generate_series_occurrences()` ignored `is_active` — **closed**

Surfaced while writing `cancel_series()`. The generator never checked the flag, so a
cancelled series would have kept producing sessions and `cancel_series()` would have been a
lie the first time anybody used it. Guard added, and asserted in the test.

Worth recording how the fix nearly went wrong: the generator was first rewritten from memory
and silently dropped the `visibility` and `source` columns and the `on conflict` clause. It
is now spliced from `pg_get_functiondef` with exactly one guard inserted. **A function
replaced "in full" should be copied from the database, not retyped.**

### A2 · `is_blocked()` had no test — **closed, and the control was already correct**

`docs/security.md` lists blocking as a control in place. The predicate is called from eight
places and nothing exercised it.

`13_blocking_test.sql` covers both directions across `profiles`, `activities`,
`nearby_activities()` and `join_activity()`, with an uninvolved third person as a control so
a policy that is merely too broad cannot pass. It also asserts the blocked person cannot
delete the block, and that unblocking restores visibility.

**The predicate was correct.** Stated plainly because it is the honest result: the control was
working, it just was not proven.

**The test was then mutation-checked.** Symmetry was removed from `is_blocked()` and the suite
re-run; it failed on the right assertion with the right message, then the mutation was
reverted. A test that has never been seen to fail is a test nobody should trust.

- **`staff` has no client grants** — still empty for both roles.

## B. Correctness — both closed

**B1** `RosterScreen` said _"N personas quedaron como no_show"_ in the close-out
confirmation. **B2** `SessionDetailScreen` carried _"No onPress yet … a hint here would
describe a flow that does not exist"_ above a report control that #29 wired up. Both now
report zero occurrences.

Both were instances of the same failure — **a statement true when written and never
re-checked** — which this repository has now had five times, counting the paraphrased
generator in A1b. It is a habit, not a series of accidents. The only defence that has
actually worked is a test that fails.

## C. Quality — open

### C1 · Client tests — **closed as a start, not as coverage**

**89 tests over six pure functions**, in `vitest`, wired into `npm run verify` and into CI as
its own step. Deliberately node-only: no jsdom, no React Native preset, no component
rendering. Testing a screen in Node means mocking the platform, and a suite built on mocks
tells you the mocks agree with each other.

**The suite found the bug the audit predicted.** `parseCoordinates` read the wrong half of a
Google Maps URL: `@lat,lng` is the map viewport, `!3d…!4d…` is the pin. At a wide zoom the
viewport is a rounded, city-level point, so two venues opened at the same zoom in the same
area produce URLs whose `@` values agree to several decimals while the pins are kilometres
apart — the exact symptom of P1 · 9. The pin pattern now runs first.

It also found two defects nobody was looking for:

- `heatColor(NaN)` **threw**. `clamp01` let NaN through, `STOPS[NaN]` was `undefined`, and the
  throw happened inside a list cell — taking down a screen rather than a card. Guarded.
- `occupancyLabel` and `densityOf` disagreed about `max = 0`: one treated it as uncapped, the
  other printed `"5 de 0"`. Unreachable behind the column constraint, and a trap regardless.

**What is still not covered:** every screen. The SQL suite covers the invariants, the unit
tests cover the arithmetic, and the middle — whether a screen wires them together correctly —
is checked by a person using the app. That gap is smaller than it was and it is not closed.

**Recorded, not fixed:** `pricePerPerson` rounds up in minor units and then renders CRC with
no decimals, so the céntimo the ceil adds is rounded away — ₡10,000 split three ways leaves
the organizer a colón short. No call sites yet, and whether the round-up is a guarantee is a
decision rather than a correction.

### C2 · `toApiError` — **closed**

One definition, in `src/lib/errors.ts`. `activities.ts` re-exports `ApiError` so existing
import sites keep working.

### C3 · The voice — **closed**

Settled as `tú`. **Zero voseo occurrences in `src/`**, down from 39 across 14 files. Converted
phrase by phrase rather than by verb-ending regex, because _"Entrás a la lista y ocupás el
lugar"_ needs two different corrections in one sentence and a regex gets one of them.

`docs/product.md` now records it as applied, with the reason restated — the pull towards `vos`
returns every time somebody writes a screen in San José, and the paragraph has to argue for
itself, not just declare.

## D. Process

### D1 · The default-privileges trap — documented, not preventable

Supabase's `alter default privileges in schema public grant all on tables to anon,
authenticated` is still in force. **Every table created after `0003` is world-readable on
creation.**

`0012` was the only migration since `0003` to add a table, and it revokes. But that is
discipline, not a mechanism: the next one is equally exposed and nothing will complain.

**Worth doing:** a test that enumerates every table and asserts its grants are intentional
would turn this from a habit into a gate. Not written.

### D2 · CI covers the database well and the client barely

The quality gate — Prettier, ESLint, `tsc --strict` — caught none of the findings in this
audit. Every one came from reading the schema, running a query, or a SQL test. That is the
argument for C1, and the reason the fourteen SQL suites are worth what they cost.

Nothing in this audit. What remains is roadmap work, and it divides cleanly by who can do it:

**Needs the Supabase dashboard — Alejandro or Kristopher:**

  they do not own.
- Storage buckets locked down; they are public by default.
- App Check / attestation.
- Close report `a0850abd` through `/staff/reportes`, not through SQL — a direct `update` skips
  the stamp and tells the reporter nothing.

**Needs a decision first:**

- Notification delivery outside the app. Email depends on the SMTP above; Web Push needs a
  service worker and VAPID keys; native push needs compiled apps. **Verify ADR 0003's claim
  that Expo notifications work on web before relying on it** — Expo's push service targets iOS
  and Android.

**Buildable now, in rough order of what it protects:**

- EXIF stripping on upload, before any feature that uploads.
- Attendance history and its 90-day delete job — together, or neither, because
  `docs/security.md` promises the job.
- The power to _act_ on a report: cancel a session, hide a profile, block an account. Three
  separate powers, three functions, three decisions about who holds them.
- A test enumerating every table's grants, which would turn D1 from a habit into a gate.
- A screen-reader pass. Every accessibility claim in this repository is a claim about code.
