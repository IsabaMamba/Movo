Verdict up front

As described — matching + social feed + group challenges + coach marketplace, built part-time, pre-launch, for a country of 5.2 million — this fails. Not because the idea is bad, but because it is four businesses with four different cold-start problems and one part-time founder.

There is a real, fundable, regional-scale business inside it. It is not the one you designed. It sells to organizers and venues, not to individuals, and it exists because Costa Rica's run-club scene went from ten people to two hundred and fifty in eighteen months with no tooling whatsoever.

01

What I believe you are building

A Spanish-language mobile app for urban Costa Rican adults roughly 18–40 that treats exercise as the pretext and belonging as the payoff. It does four things: matches people to train together, wraps a social feed around logged workouts, runs group challenges for accountability, and connects users to coaches and classes.

The problem it claims to solve is real and measurable. Roughly half of Costa Rican adults fall below the WHO minimum of 150 weekly minutes, and the Plideco study of the GAM puts sedentarism at 51%. Meanwhile gyms have overtaken football as the country's top physical activity — 33.6% of active people in the GAM go to a gym versus 21.4% who play football — and the same study notes the migration from public plazas into gyms is driven by insecurity, weather, and decayed public infrastructure, producing a fitness culture of individual consumption rather than community recreation. So: more people exercising, more of them alone, in a country where half of everyone does nothing. The gap you're pointing at exists.

Assumptions I am making

  • A1You are solo or near-solo, unfunded, and working on this part-time alongside a degree and a job.
  • A2The stack is React / React Native on Firebase — your existing toolkit.
  • A3Zero users, zero waitlist, no signed gym or club partners, no letters of intent.
  • A4Spanish-first, GAM-first, iOS + Android eventually.
  • A5You have not yet decided whether romantic matching is allowed. It will happen anyway.
  • A6"Regional" means Costa Rica first, then Panama / Guatemala / El Salvador.

If A3 is wrong — if you already have a gym or a club willing to pilot — several conclusions below get considerably more optimistic. Tell me and I'll redo the growth and moat sections.

The single most consequential thing you told me: the core mechanic is “all of them.” That is not ambition. It is four cold-start problems stacked on one part-time founder, and it is the most likely cause of death.

02

Vulnerabilities, worst first

Ranked by what can actually kill the company, not by how uncomfortable they are to read. Severity is damage if it happens; likelihood is my estimate given where you are today.

V01

Four products, one part-time founder

CriticalNear-certain
Problem
Matching, a feed, challenges, and a marketplace are four separate businesses. Matching needs two-sided local liquidity. A feed needs network density and content velocity. Challenges need retention mechanics. A marketplace needs supply acquisition, payments, and trust. Each has a different "first 100 users."
Why
Well-funded teams have died attempting any one of them. Splitting a part-time founder's attention four ways guarantees that none reaches the quality threshold where users switch away from what they already use.
Fix
Choose one mechanic this week. My recommendation is coordination of recurring group sessions — everything else is a distraction until that works in one district.
V02

Density, not reach — the matching math doesn't close

CriticalHigh
Problem
A matching product is worthless below a local density threshold. National user counts are irrelevant; what matters is how many people are available at one venue, in one time slot, on one day.
Why
The arithmetic is unforgiving at Costa Rica's scale:
// Serviceable pool, honestly derived
Costa Rica population ~5.2 M
Gran Área Metropolitana (~50%) ~2.6 M
Ages 18–40 (~33%) ~860 K
× physically active (~49%) ~420 K
× gym-goers (33.6% of active) ~145 K ← your SAM

// What 2% penetration actually feels like
2% of 145 K 2,900 users
÷ ~40 viable venues in the GAM 72 per venue
× ~15% active in any given week 11 per venue
÷ ~6 realistic training time slots < 2 per slot ← nobody matches
Fix
Launch one district, one activity, one recurring time slot — not a country. Concentrate 200 users into one place rather than 5,000 across a nation. Everything about your roadmap changes once you accept this.
V03

Your real competitor is a free WhatsApp group

CriticalNear-certain
Problem
Costa Rica's run clubs went from under ten participants to over 250 in roughly eighteen months — No Corro Nada, Un Club Secreto, Rise and Run — coordinated entirely through Instagram and WhatsApp, with DJs, cafés, and sponsors attached. Zero product. Zero cost. 100% installed base.
Why
You are not competing with an app; you are competing with a habit that already works. Asking a 250-person group to move to a new app is asking the organizer to take on migration risk for a benefit they haven't felt yet. Feature count is not persuasion here — it is friction.
Fix
Don't ask anyone to leave WhatsApp. Sit beside it: give the organizer the things WhatsApp genuinely cannot do — attendance history, rosters, waivers, route archives, and a sponsor report they can invoice against — and let members keep chatting where they already are.
V04

Fix
Monetize supply, not demand. The consumer app becomes free distribution, not the revenue line. See §08.
V05

Physical-safety liability in a one-media-cycle country

CriticalLow–Medium
Problem
You are introducing strangers who then meet in physical space, often early morning or after dark, in a country where perceived insecurity is already documented as the reason people moved their exercise indoors. One serious incident traceable to the app ends the company.
Why
Likelihood per meeting is low; consequence is unbounded and non-recoverable. In a market of five million with a single national news cycle, there is no "quiet incident."
Fix
Before a single stranger meets a stranger: identity verification (cédula or phone + selfie), groups of three or more by default with 1:1 disabled, public and named venues only, no precise real-time location sharing, in-app reporting with a named human responder, block/ban tooling, and a written incident protocol. Ship these with the matching feature, never after it.
V06

Strava is shipping your feature set right now

HighHigh
Problem
Strava has ~180 M registered users, passed one million clubs, saw running clubs grow 3.5× year-on-year and club-organized events 1.5×, and has announced a club events tab, sub-group route sharing, and club-only leaderboards for the second half of 2026.
Why
That is your feed and half your community layer, shipped by an incumbent that already has your users' data and their friends. Competing on that surface is unwinnable.
Fix
Never build a feed. Integrate: read-only Strava OAuth so a session logged in your app appears in the user's existing Strava life. Be the layer Strava won't build — Costa Rican venue relationships, local operations, sponsor reporting, and Spanish-language ground truth.
V07

Ley 8968 — fitness data is legally sensitive data

HighMedium–High
Problem
Costa Rica's Ley 8968 classifies health data as sensitive and requires express written consent for it — an unsigned digital acknowledgment does not suffice. Processing sensitive data improperly is a serious or very serious infraction, carrying 5–30 base salaries (roughly ₡2.3 M–₡13.9 M in 2026). Personal data may leave Costa Rica only where PRODHAB has found adequacy or a derogation applies — directly relevant if you host on US-region Firebase.
Why
Weight, injuries, goals, heart rate and training history are health data. Most CR consumer apps ignore this and get away with it until they matter. A company selling to corporates or taking brand money is exactly the kind that gets asked.
Fix
Collect the minimum — you need a sport, a level and availability, not a body-fat percentage. Granular, separate, explicit consent in Spanish for anything health-adjacent. A written privacy notice and data map. Set Firebase to a defensible region or document your transfer basis. Budget a few hours of local counsel; it's cheap compared to the fine range.
V08

Client-side authorization on Firebase

CriticalHigh
Problem
The standard failure mode of a solo-built Firebase prototype: permissive Firestore rules, business logic enforced in the client, and a users collection made broadly readable because matching "needs" it.
Why
Anything the client can read, anyone can read. A matching app's user collection contains names, photos, schedules and neighbourhoods — a package that is genuinely dangerous when combined with V05.
Fix
Deny-by-default rules with unit tests in the emulator; a separate minimal public profile document containing only what matching truly needs; every privileged operation behind a Cloud Function; App Check enabled before launch. Details in §10.
V09

Two of the highest-churn categories, combined

HighHigh
Problem
Fitness apps and social-discovery apps both suffer brutal cohort collapse — fitness through motivation decay, discovery through "I found who I needed, goodbye." Stacking them doesn't average the churn; it compounds it.
Why
A successful match is a reason to stop opening your app. That is a structural problem, not a notifications problem.
Fix
Make the retained object a recurring scheduled commitment with named people — the standing Tuesday 6 p.m. — not a streak, a badge, or a match. The app must be the place the next session is confirmed, or it will be closed after the first one.
V10

SINPE Móvil erases any take rate

HighNear-certain
Problem
SINPE Móvil has ~4.6 million active subscriptions in a country of 5.2 million, moved ₡12.5 trillion across 747 million transactions in 2025, and costs users nothing. Any fee you insert between two Costa Ricans gets routed around in one WhatsApp message.
Why
Marketplace and transaction-fee models assume you own the payment rail. Here, the rail is free, instant, universal, and outside your control. Hacienda is also tightening fiscal scrutiny of SINPE Móvil flows — another reason not to sit in the money path.
Fix
Charge for software and access, never for moving money. Subscriptions and sponsorship packages, invoiced with proper factura electrónica.
V11

Marketplace supply has no reason to join

HighHigh
Problem
Costa Rican trainers already have Instagram for audience and SINPE for payment. You offer neither an audience they lack nor a payment method they need, and you'd take a cut of both.
Why
Marketplaces are won by whoever brings demand. You have none. This is the feature most likely to consume months and yield nothing.
Fix
Cut it from the designs today. Revisit only when you own an audience a coach cannot reach otherwise.
V12

Founder capacity is the binding constraint

CriticalHigh
Problem
This is a business that wins on local operations — showing up at clubs, standing at gym doors, chasing organizers — and it is competing for your hours against a job, an engineering degree, design coursework, and other ventures.
Why
Capital isn't your constraint and neither is code — you can build this. Sustained weekly presence in one district for ninety days is the constraint, and it is the one thing that cannot be batched, automated, or done at 11 p.m.
Fix
Write down the real number of hours per week you can defend for six months. If it is under ten, either recruit a co-founder who owns community and operations, or choose the version of this that is a tool rather than a network — the organizer-facing product needs far fewer hours than the consumer network does.
V13

Gym culture in the GAM is individual on purpose

HighMedium
Problem
The same study that shows gyms overtaking football attributes the shift to safety, weather and equipment — people moved indoors partly to be left alone. "Meet strangers at your gym" may be selling against the reason they chose the gym.
Fix
Target contexts where joining strangers is already the norm — group classes, run clubs, court sports, pickup football — rather than the weight room floor.
V14

It becomes a dating app whether you allow it or not

Medium–HighMedium–High
Problem
Any app that matches young adults for in-person meetings acquires a romantic use case. Playtomic, a padel booking app, has marriages attributed to it.
Why
If you become known as "el Tinder del gym," you inherit dating-app moderation costs and safety exposure while losing the people who came to train, plus the gyms and brands who won't associate with it.
Fix
Decide the policy before launch and encode it in the product: group-first, activity-first, no swipe interaction, no "looking for" field, no profile browsing without a session context. Ambiguity here is a decision to become a dating app by default.
V15

Platform and vendor dependency

MediumMedium
Problem
App store commissions of 15–30% on any consumer subscription; Firebase cost cliffs once reads scale with a social graph; Strava and Apple Health API terms that can change unilaterally; WhatsApp Business API pricing if you build on it.
Fix
Web-first for anything transactional so payments avoid store fees; model Firestore reads per session before you have traffic; treat any third-party integration as a convenience layer that the product survives losing.
03

Windows of opportunity

Ordered by how well they fit a part-time founder targeting a regional business. The first two are, in my view, considerably better businesses than the one you designed.

Opportunity 01 — strongest

The run-club operating system

Why now
CR run clubs grew from under 10 to 250+ attendees in ~18 months. Brands are now approaching organizers directly — and organizers have no attendance data, no roster, no waiver, no proof of delivery to invoice against. The money arrived before the tooling did.
Upside
20–60 CR clubs at ₡25–45K/mo, plus every club's roster as free consumer distribution, plus a path into sponsorship brokerage. Replicable in Panamá and Guatemala with identical dynamics.
Difficulty
Low–medium. Single-sided. No liquidity problem.
Cheap test
Run attendance and a one-page sponsor report by hand for one club for four weeks. Then stop, and see whether the organizer asks for it back.
Opportunity 02 — structural

Sports that structurally require other people

Why now
Playtomic — 4 M users, 66 countries, a 0–7 level algorithm, open matches with strangers — already operates in Costa Rica through Urban Sport, Park & Padel and Play Pádel Tamarindo. It proves the model, and it proves the boundary: padel cannot be played alone. Running can. That's why padel matching works and gym matching struggles.
Upside
The sports Playtomic doesn't touch in CR: mejengas, basketball, volleyball, surf, group cycling. Enormous participation, coordinated entirely by WhatsApp, no tooling, and a scarce bookable resource (the cancha) to anchor monetization.
Difficulty
Medium. Needs venue supply, but venues have an obvious incentive: empty slots.
Cheap test
A "completar la mejenga" broker for five canchas, run over WhatsApp by you, personally, for two weeks. Count sessions that happened only because you filled them.
Opportunity 03 — unfair advantage

Corporate wellness, using the door you already have

Why now
Wellhub and TotalPass sell gym access to LatAm employers. Nobody sells team activity and belonging for hybrid workforces — which is the actual complaint HR has. Costa Rica's free-zone and services employers have budgets and measurable wellness mandates.
Upside
Contract revenue instead of consumer churn, per-employee pricing, and a logo that unlocks the next five. This is the fastest path to a real regional number.
Difficulty
Medium — but you work inside a large employer, which is a distribution advantage most founders would pay for.
Cheap test
Run a six-week team challenge for one internal team off a spreadsheet. Measure weekly participation, not signups.
Opportunity 04 — positioning

Safety-first group training for women

Why now
The documented driver pushing Costa Ricans indoors is perceived insecurity in public space. For women that constraint is sharper and largely unaddressed — the barrier to a 5 a.m. run is not motivation.
Upside
Turns your biggest liability (V05) into your positioning, produces unusually strong word-of-mouth, and gives brands a story worth sponsoring. Narrow enough to reach density in one district.
Difficulty
Medium. Demands genuine verification and moderation investment — which you needed anyway.
Cheap test
Two verified group runs in one district, co-hosted with an existing organizer. Measure return rate at week 3, not attendance at week 1.
Opportunity 05

Sponsorship brokerage with verified attendance

Why now
Brands are already contacting individual run clubs. Nobody can sell them twelve clubs at once with verified head counts and demographics.
Upside
Marketing budgets are an order of magnitude above what consumers pay. Attendance data becomes a genuine proprietary asset — see §06.
Difficulty
Medium–high. Requires club supply first; this is the second move, not the first.
Cheap test
Sell one ₡150K package to one local brand for one month across two clubs. Deliver a real report.
Opportunity 06

Independent gyms losing to Smart Fit

Why now
Smart Fit is expanding aggressively across the GAM. Independent gyms cannot compete on price or equipment; community and retention are the only ground left to them, and they have no software for it.
Upside
A defined, motivated, reachable set of buyers with an existential reason to say yes.
Difficulty
Medium. Long-ish sales cycle, small ticket, but honest recurring revenue.
Cheap test
Ten in-person conversations with independent gym owners in the GAM. Ask what they'd pay to cut monthly churn by a fifth.
04

If I took over as CEO today

First act: delete three of the four mechanics from the designs, permanently, before writing another line of code. The positioning becomes "the tool that makes group training actually happen in your neighbourhood" — and the customer becomes the person who organizes, not the person who attends.

Must do

  • Cut to one mechanic: recurring group sessions at real venues.
  • Change the customer to the organizer / venue.
  • Pick one district and one activity. Nothing national.
  • Ship a mobile web app first — no store review, no store fees, no dual-platform tax.
  • Identity verification and group-only meetings, from day one.
  • Write the safety protocol before the first meetup.
  • Spanish-first copy, written for ticos, not translated.
  • Seed the map with real public club sessions so it is never empty.
  • Set a 90-day kill criterion in writing.

Should do

  • Read-only Strava OAuth, so users keep their existing habit.
  • Attendance history and roster export for organizers.
  • A one-page sponsor report generated automatically.
  • WhatsApp deep links for invites — meet users where they are.
  • One paid pilot with a gym, club or cancha within 60 days.
  • Session reminders 12 h and 1 h before. Nothing else notifies.
  • Ley 8968 consent flow and privacy notice in Spanish.

Could do

  • A skill-level system, once you have enough sessions to calibrate it.
  • Waivers and liability release per session.
  • Post-session photo archive — the actual reason people share.
  • Referral: an organizer who brings another organizer.
  • Rain contingency for the May–November season.

Don't do

  • A social feed. Strava wins this and is shipping more of it now.
  • A coach marketplace. No demand to offer, no payment gap to fill.
  • In-app payments. SINPE eats your take rate.
  • Streaks, badges, points. They don't retain adults.
  • An AI coach or chatbot. Nobody is switching apps for it.
  • Wearable integrations at this stage.
  • Native iOS + Android before product-market fit.
  • Swipe-based matching of any kind.
  • A second city before the first has 40% four-week retention.

07

Growth

Best initial customer: not a user — an organizer. Specifically, a run club or cancha in the GAM with 50–250 recurring participants, an active Instagram, and at least one sponsor conversation already underway. There are perhaps 30–60 of them in the country and you can name most within a week.

Cheapest channel: your legs. Twenty in-person visits to clubs, canchas and independent gyms. Cost is zero and the information is worth more than any ad you could buy.

Most scalable channel: organizer-led distribution. One organizer brings their entire roster in a single message — the only real growth loop in this business. Consumer-by-consumer acquisition in a market this size will never compound.

The loop, stated plainly: organizer adopts the tool → roster joins to see the schedule → attendance data accumulates → data becomes a sponsor report → sponsorship makes the organizer visibly more successful → other organizers ask what they're using.

Channels that will not work yet: paid acquisition (no LTV to pay against), SEO (search volume in CR is negligible for this), influencers (expensive relative to just talking to the twelve people who matter).

Technical & security review

Assessed against the likely React / React Native + Firebase architecture. Described defensively — what the weakness is and how to close it.

RankIssueWhy it matters & how to remediate
CriticalAuthorization enforced in the clientIf Firestore rules are permissive and the app decides what a user may see, then anyone can read everything regardless of the UI. Remediate with deny-by-default rules, rules unit tests in the emulator as part of your normal workflow, and every privileged operation behind a Cloud Function with server-side checks.
CriticalOver-broad user documentsMatching tempts you to make the users collection widely readable. That collection holds names, photos, neighbourhoods and schedules — a package that is genuinely dangerous for meeting strangers. Split into a minimal public profile (display name, sport, level) and a private document; never expose the private one to clients.
CriticalPrecise location storageStoring exact coordinates or location history creates both a safety hazard and a Ley 8968 exposure. Store venue IDs and district-level areas only. Never real-time position, never location history.
HighNo App Check / no abuse controlsWithout attestation, your backend is an open API: the entire user base can be enumerated by anyone who reads your bundle. Enable App Check before launch; rate-limit invites, messages and profile reads server-side.
HighSMS-only authenticationPhone-only auth makes account takeover a SIM-swap away, and an account here carries real-world meeting privileges. Add a second factor or an email fallback for account recovery; re-verify on device change; alert on new-device sign-in.
HighNo moderation or reporting pipelineReporting, blocking, and a human who actually reads reports are not v2 features for a product that arranges physical meetings. Build report → auto-suspend threshold → human review, and log every action with an audit trail before launch.
HighCross-border transfer without a basisCosta Rican personal data may leave the country only under an adequacy finding or a valid derogation. Select a defensible Firebase region, document the transfer basis, and reflect it in the privacy notice.
HighSecrets in the client bundleAnything shipped to a device is public: third-party keys, admin endpoints, webhook URLs. Keep every secret server-side in Functions config; rotate anything that has ever been committed to a repo.
MediumUnvalidated user contentProfile text, session descriptions and images render in other users' views. Validate and sanitize server-side; scan uploads; strip EXIF from photos, which otherwise carry GPS coordinates straight into a stranger's hands.
MediumNo backup or export pathOnce clubs keep attendance history with you, losing it is a business-ending event and a contractual one. Scheduled exports, tested restores, and a documented recovery procedure.

Ten things you're most likely overlooking

  1. Your competitor is WhatsApp, it is free, it is already installed, and it is currently winning with 250 people at a time.
  2. Connection is an outcome, not a feature. You cannot ship belonging. You can only ship a reliable recurring event, and belonging happens on its own around week four.
  3. The organizer is the customer. There are ~40 of them in the GAM and each carries a roster. You have been designing for the roster.
  4. Density beats reach. Two hundred users in Escazú is a functioning product; five thousand spread across Costa Rica is an empty screen.
  5. Rainy season is half the year. May to November wrecks outdoor participation, and your retention charts will look like a collapsing business every June unless you plan indoor alternatives and read seasonality correctly.
  6. Health data is legally sensitive here. A checkbox is not consent under Ley 8968, and the sanctions are denominated in base salaries, not in dollars you can ignore.
  7. A safety incident is existential, not manageable, in a country with one national news cycle and five million people.
  8. Gym-goers moved indoors partly to be alone. You may be selling against the reason they chose the gym in the first place.
  9. The moment a company pays you, Hacienda exists. Factura electrónica, tax registration, and a legal entity are prerequisites for B2B revenue — and Hacienda is currently tightening scrutiny of SINPE Móvil flows specifically.
  10. Attention is your scarcest input, not money and not engineering. This is at least your fifth concurrent project. The version of this business that survives is the one designed around ten focused hours a week, not the one that assumes forty.

A bonus eleventh, because investors will ask it first: "why hasn't a run club just built this?" Have an answer. The honest one is that organizers are community people, not software people — which is exactly why the tool has to come from outside and exactly why they'll pay for it.

12

Kill the idea

The strongest case that this fails

  1. The problem is already solved, for free, by unpaid people doing it better every month. Instagram plus WhatsApp took a run club from ten to 250 in eighteen months. Your app has to beat that, and "beating" a free tool everyone already has requires being ten times better at something, not slightly better at four things.
  2. The market is arithmetically too small for the model you chose. A consumer subscription against 145,000 realistic prospects cannot produce a salary, let alone a company. You picked a regional scope, which is a defensible choice — but it is incompatible with consumer-scale monetization.
  3. Four mechanics from a part-time founder means shipping none of them well enough to switch anyone. The bar is not "works." The bar is "better than the WhatsApp group I'm already in."
  4. The incumbents are moving into this exact space now. Strava is shipping club events, route sharing and club leaderboards in H2 2026. Playtomic has already solved social matching for the one sport where matching is structurally necessary, and is already in Costa Rica.
  5. There is no moat and no supply. No exclusive venues, no proprietary data, no distribution advantage, no switching cost. Everything in the current design is copyable in a quarter.
  6. The one input that would fix all of the above — dense, sustained local operations — is the input a part-time founder with four other commitments structurally cannot provide.

What would have to be true for it to work anyway

  1. You cut to one mechanic and one activity type within the week.
  2. The customer becomes the organizer or the venue, not the individual.
  3. You can personally be present in one district, every week, for ninety days.
  4. Revenue comes from supply and brands, not consumer subscriptions.
  5. You reach real liquidity in one micro-market before touching a second.
  6. Safety and Ley 8968 are handled before the first stranger meets a stranger.
  7. You can defend fifteen focused hours a week for six months.

Items 1, 3 and 7 are the load-bearing ones. If those three are not true, no amount of design quality, engineering skill, or feature work will save this — and you would be better off putting those hours into one of your other ventures.

Would I keep building it?

Yes — but not this version, and not without a deadline.

I'd keep it because the underlying observation is correct and unusually well-timed. Half the country is sedentary, the fitness culture that's growing is a lonely one, an enormous grassroots wave of run clubs appeared in eighteen months, brands are pointing money at it, and nobody has built the tooling. That is a genuine window and it is open right now. You are also, unusually, capable of executing every function this needs alone.

I'd refuse to build this version because it's four products, its customer is the wrong person, its revenue model is arithmetically dead in a country of five million, and it has no moat. Those aren't fixable by iteration — they're fixable only by deciding differently, this week.

And I'd put a date on it. Ninety days, one district, one activity, one paying organizer or a hundred weekly-active participants. If neither exists on day ninety, I would close it without regret and put the hours into whichever of your other ventures is showing more pull. The most expensive outcome here isn't failing — it's spending two part-time years on something that never gets a clear answer because it never got a clear test.
